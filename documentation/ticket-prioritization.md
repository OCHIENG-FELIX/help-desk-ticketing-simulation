# Ticket Prioritization

## Overview
Ticket prioritization helps IT support teams determine which incidents require attention first.

Priority is generally determined by evaluating the impact of the incident and its urgency.

## Impact
Impact measures how many users, systems, or business operations are affected.

Examples:
- High: An entire department cannot access a critical system.
- Medium: Several users experience a service interruption.
- Low: One user experiences a minor issue.

## Urgency
Urgency measures how quickly the issue needs to be resolved to prevent further disruption.

Examples:
- High: A critical business service is unavailable.
- Medium: A user cannot complete an important task.
- Low: A minor issue has a reasonable workaround.

## Priority Levels

| Priority | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| P1 - Critical | Major business service disruption | Organization-wide network outage |
| P2 - High | Significant impact on operations | Department unable to access essential application |
| P3 - Medium | Limited impact | One employee cannot access email |
| P4 - Low | Minor issue or routine request | Request for software information |

## Prioritization Considerations
- Number of affected users.
- Importance of the affected service.
- Business impact.
- Availability of a workaround.
- Time sensitivity.

## Important Note
Priority should follow the organization's approved impact and urgency matrix. A single user's issue may still be high priority if it affects a critical business function.

## Objective
Ensure that incidents are handled according to business impact and urgency rather than simply the order in which tickets are received.
