# Service Level Agreement (SLA) Management

## Overview
A Service Level Agreement defines the expected level of service between a service provider and its customer or organization.

In IT support, SLAs commonly establish targets for responding to and resolving incidents.

## Key SLA Measurements

### 1. Response Time
The time between reporting an incident and the support team providing its initial response.

### 2. Resolution Time
The time taken to restore service or resolve the reported issue.

### 3. Availability
The proportion of time that a service is available for use.

## Example SLA Targets

The following are illustrative targets, not universal industry standards.

| Priority | Initial Response Target | Resolution Target |
|---|---|---|
| P1 - Critical | 15 minutes | 4 hours |
| P2 - High | 30 minutes | 8 hours |
| P3 - Medium | 4 hours | 2 business days |
| P4 - Low | 1 business day | 5 business days |

Actual targets depend on the organization's SLA.

## SLA Monitoring
IT support teams should:
- Record ticket creation time.
- Track first response time.
- Monitor resolution progress.
- Identify approaching SLA deadlines.
- Escalate overdue incidents.
- Document reasons for delays.

## Importance
SLA management improves accountability, service quality, communication, and customer satisfaction.
