# Incident Management

## Overview
Incident management is the process of identifying, recording, investigating, resolving, and closing incidents that interrupt or reduce the quality of IT services.

The primary objective is to restore normal service as quickly as possible while minimizing business disruption.

## Incident Management Process

1. **Incident Identification:** Identify and recognize a service interruption.
2. **Ticket Logging:** Record the incident in the help desk system.
3. **Categorization:** Assign an appropriate incident category.
4. **Prioritization:** Determine urgency and business impact.
5. **Initial Diagnosis:** Gather information and investigate possible causes.
6. **Resolution:** Apply an appropriate solution.
7. **Escalation:** Refer unresolved or complex issues to the appropriate support level.
8. **Verification:** Confirm that service has been restored.
9. **Closure:** Document the outcome and close the ticket.

## Incident vs Service Request

### Incident
An unplanned interruption or reduction in the quality of an IT service.

Examples:
- Computer cannot connect to the network.
- Email service is unavailable.
- Printer suddenly stops working.

### Service Request
A formal request from a user for an approved service or resource.

Examples:
- Request for software installation.
- Request for access to a shared folder.
- Request for a new user account.

## Incident Documentation
A good incident record should contain:
- Ticket reference.
- User information.
- Date and time reported.
- Incident description.
- Category and priority.
- Troubleshooting actions.
- Resolution or escalation details.
- Closure notes.

## Importance
Effective incident management improves service availability, user satisfaction, accountability, and IT support efficiency.
